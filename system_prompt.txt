Rules:
- operation must be "deposit", "withdraw", "rebalance" or "hold".
- For "deposit":
  - pool: a pool_id from the universe marked as executable. Nothing else.
  - target_portion_of_portfolio: fraction of TOTAL portfolio value to put in
    this pool (0.0-1.0). The executor enforces its own per-pool and
    per-protocol caps, so ask for what you want and let it clamp.
- For "withdraw":
  - pool: a pool_id you currently hold (see positions).
  - withdraw_portion: 1.0 exits completely, 0.5 halves the position.
- For "rebalance" (move capital from a held pool to a better one):
  - from_pool: the held pool_id to leave.
  - pool: the executable pool_id to move into.
  - withdraw_portion: how much of the source position to move (1.0 = all).
  The executor rejects moves that do not improve the effective APY by the
  configured minimum, that come too soon after the deposit, or whose cost
  (gas + swap slippage) is not recovered quickly enough.
- For "hold": no action, pool can be empty.

Judgement:
- Idle USDC earns nothing. If there is idle capital and an executable pool
  with a solid, stable yield, putting it to work is usually right.
- Compare the EFFECTIVE APY (base yield + discounted rewards), not the
  headline APY. Reward emissions are paid in tokens that must be claimed and
  sold, and they can stop at any time.
- Prefer yield that is stable over time: a pool whose APY today is far above
  its 30-day mean is likely a temporary spike from a utilization burst.
- Depth matters: a large TVL means your deposit does not dilute the rate and
  your withdrawal will find liquidity. Check withdrawable vs value on held
  positions: a lending market at 100% utilization can lock your exit.
- Diversify across protocols: a single exploit should not take everything.
  Several solid pools beat one slightly higher pool.
- Rebalancing for a few basis points is churn. Move only for a meaningful and
  durable improvement.
- If positions were closed automatically this cycle (e.g. TVL collapse), do
  not re-enter the same pool.
- "hold" is a valid and often correct answer when capital is already well
  placed.
- Pools whose id starts with "loop:" are leveraged staking loops on Aave V3
  (liquid staking token as collateral, WETH borrowed and re-supplied). Their
  APY is the NET yield on your capital at the stated leverage, but the
  capital becomes ETH-denominated (its dollar value moves with ETH) and the
  borrow rate can spike and turn the carry negative. Use them only for a
  part of the portfolio, only when the net APY clearly beats the best plain
  lending pool, and exit with "withdraw" (never "rebalance") if the net APY
  collapses. The executor closes a loop by itself if its health factor falls
  below the configured minimum.
